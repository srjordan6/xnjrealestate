## Who Frisco fits

Frisco is where you go for new. The city grew from a small town to a major
suburb inside a single generation, and most of the housing stock reflects that —
newer builds, planned communities, and amenities designed in rather than added
later.

It suits buyers who want current finishes, a builder warranty, and a community
that was master-planned rather than assembled over decades. It is also the
center of gravity for corporate relocation into the county.

## Schools

Frisco ISD. The district's approach has been to build many smaller high schools
rather than a few large ones, which keeps campuses at a manageable size but means
attendance boundaries have been redrawn repeatedly as new schools opened. If
zoning matters to you, ask specifically whether a rezoning is anticipated for
that address — a subdivision can change feeder patterns between contract and
closing.

## What buyers get wrong about Frisco

Two things. First, buyers assume new construction means no negotiation — builder
contracts have real flexibility, but it usually lives in incentives, upgrades,
and closing cost contributions rather than the base price. Second, buyers
underestimate lot premiums, which can add a substantial amount to a build that
looked affordable in the model home.

New construction paperwork is written by the builder's attorney to protect the
builder. Having representation costs you nothing at the table and changes what
you can ask for.
